Why me

Why brands should test me

Five specific reasons, none of which require a portfolio of past work.

01

I use this stuff for a living, not for a video

Nineteen years of residential HVAC. Six or seven houses a day. My van runs on one battery platform because I picked it and stuck with it, not because someone sent me a box.

02

My job is convincing a sceptical person to spend money

That is the whole job of an ad. I have done it in person, thousands of times, to homeowners who did not want to hear the number.

03

I am the customer your ads are aimed at

Working trade, mid-forties, Midwest. If you sell tools, work wear or home comfort products, I am not playing your buyer. I am him.

04

Locations you cannot rent

A real workshop with a real tool wall, a working service van, actual attics and crawlspaces, a backyard and a garage. No set dressing.

05

I undersell

I do not do hype. If a product is fine but not worth the extra forty dollars, that is what I will say. That is usually why people believe me.

Voiceover · no face

“This came out of a house I was at this morning. The homeowner said the air upstairs never felt right.”

Air filters · indoor air quality

A filthy used filter next to a clean one. Plain explanation of what the dirty one was doing to the system and to the power bill, then how often people should really be changing them. No pitch until the last five seconds.

Shot list
  1. Close on the dirty filter, side light, held in a work glove
  2. Clean filter entering frame beside it
  3. Close on the pleats, tilt to show the dust
  4. Wide of the return vent in a hallway
  5. Hands sliding the new filter in, click of the cover
  6. Static hold on both filters for the end card
Durability · long-term test

“This tumbler has been in attics with me for five years. I want to show you what it looks like now.”

Drinkware · coolers · worksite gear

The scratches, the dents, the faded print. Then ice in at 6am and the lid off at the end of a work day. The proof is the ice, not the adjectives.

Shot list
  1. Tumbler on the tailgate, morning light, close on the wear
  2. Ice going in, sound up
  3. Attic hatch and ladder, heat shimmer if I can get it
  4. Mid-day cutaway, working, tumbler in frame
  5. End of day, lid off, ice still there
  6. Eight-second talking head: would I buy it again
Talking head · single take

“I can tell in about thirty seconds if the last guy sold you a part you didn't need. It's always the same tell.”

Home comfort · service tools · positioning

The tell is one component newer than everything around it. Then what a homeowner should ask instead. Consumer-advocate framing, works with or without a product in it.

Shot list
  1. Truck cab, phone on the dash mount, single take
  2. B-roll of a furnace with one obviously newer part
  3. Close on my hand pointing at the mismatch
  4. Back to the cab for the what-to-ask-instead line
